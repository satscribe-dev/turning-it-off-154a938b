Entry for the [make.fun bounty "Have You Tried Turning It Off?"](https://make.fun/bounty/u-motasimfuad-have-you-tried-turning-it-off-154a938b): a program that writes one funny single-line reply from a hopeless tech support agent to a desperate customer.

## Run

```sh
node main.ts   # writes /output/entry.txt
```

Deterministic: two runs produce byte-identical output. No network, no input files.

## The entry (93 chars)

> I'm escalating this to Tier 2 — that's me again, but with a deeper voice and the same script.

## Candidates & pairwise bracket

Eight candidates were generated, then compared pairwise against the judge's criteria (on-subject single line; unexpected twist; cheerful uselessness; customer never the butt; sounds like a real agent; tight; fresh / twists the classic).

1. "Have you tried turning it off and on again? No rush — I'll just sit here billing you by the minute."
2. "Sir, I understand you're locked out of everything — have you tried turning your life off and on again?"
3. "Great news: your computer isn't broken, it's just taking a well-earned break from you."
4. **"I'm escalating this to Tier 2 — that's me again, but with a deeper voice and the same script."** ← winner
5. "Your printer says it's offline, sir — honestly, after 47 calls today, I get it."
6. "Have you tried turning it off and on again? If that fails, try turning yourself off for a bit."
7. "Please hold — I'm transferring you to a specialist. That's me, I just needed a coffee."
8. "Good news, your files are safe — they're just in the cloud now, which is my way of saying gone."

Why #4 won: #4 vs #7 — same escalation family, but "deeper voice and the same script" is a sharper, more specific twist than the coffee gag. #4 vs #2/#6 — those make the customer the butt ("your life", "yourself"), which the judge penalises. #4 vs #1 — the billing joke is stock and the classic opener is untwisted. #4 vs #5/#3/#8 — decent empathy or premise, but a milder twist; #4's reveal that the escalation IS the same agent is the most surprising turn, and the humour comes purely from the agent's cheerful uselessness.
